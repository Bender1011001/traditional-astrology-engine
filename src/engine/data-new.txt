The Reconstructed Doctrine: A Philological and Astrological Analysis of Planetary Delineations in Hellenistic and Medieval TraditionsAbstractThis report presents a comprehensive reconstruction of specific planetary delineations—Mars and Sun in the 2nd House; Saturn, Jupiter, Sun, and Venus in the 3rd House; Jupiter, Mars, Sun, and Mercury in the 4th House; Jupiter and Mercury in the 7th House; and the Sun in the 12th House—that have frequently been categorized as "undefined" or "silent" in contemporary summaries of traditional astrology. By rigorously examining the primary source texts of the Hellenistic and Medieval periods, specifically the Mathesis of Julius Firmicus Maternus (4th Century CE), the Anthology of Vettius Valens (2nd Century CE), the Compendium of Rhetorius the Egyptian (6th Century CE), and the predictive treatises of Abu Ma'shar (9th Century CE) and Guido Bonatti (13th Century CE), this study demonstrates that these placements possess explicit, textually supported prognostics. Furthermore, this report analyzes the role of the Moon in Solar Return charts and the sign-specific planetary delineations of Vettius Valens, illustrating the critical importance of sect, angularity, and planetary joys in the interpretive logic of the ancients. The findings challenge the notion of textual silence, revealing instead a complex, interwoven system of delineation that requires a synthesis of disparate astrological doctrines to be fully articulated.1. Introduction: The Problem of "Silent" SourcesThe transmission of astrological knowledge from the Hellenistic era through the Arabic translation movement to the Latin West is characterized by both remarkable continuity and significant fragmentation. Modern practitioners and historians often encounter lacunae—apparent gaps where specific interpretations for planetary placements seem missing from the available lexicon. The user query highlights a series of such placements (e.g., "SUN_2: Sources silent") and asks for the recovery of this information.The perception of silence often arises not from an absence of material, but from the organizational structure of ancient texts. Unlike modern "cookbooks" that list delineations in a linear "Planet in House" format, ancient authors like Firmicus Maternus and Vettius Valens embedded their interpretations within broader discussions of the Thema Mundi (the birth chart of the cosmos), the Oikodespotes (Master of the Nativity), the doctrine of sect (diurnal vs. nocturnal charts), and specific case studies.This report adopts a philological and technical approach to reconstruct these "missing" delineations. By triangulating references from the foundational texts of the tradition, we can recover the specific prognostics for the requested placements. The analysis indicates that the ancient logic was highly deterministic yet nuanced by condition modifiers such as sect and aspect. For instance, the "silence" regarding Mars in the 2nd House is broken by Firmicus's explicit warnings about the dissipation of substance, conditioned by the chart's sect.The report is structured into four primary sections:Theoretical Framework: Establishing the ancient logic of house division, sect, and planetary joys that underpins all delineations.Natal Delineations: A deep textual analysis of the specific natal placements requested (Houses 2, 3, 4, 7, and 12).Solar Return Moon: A reconstruction of annual predictive delineations for the Moon, synthesizing Abu Ma'shar and Volguine.Valens' Sign-Specific Delineations: An examination of the zodiacal logic found in the Anthology, addressing the request for deeper specificity.2. Theoretical Framework: The Logic of the PlacesTo understand the reconstructed delineations, one must first grasp the technical architecture of Hellenistic astrology. The meanings of the houses (or "places," topoi) were not arbitrary but were derived from their geometric relationship to the Ascendant (Horoskopos) and their association with planetary "joys."2.1 The Gate of Hades and the AversionsThe Second House, known as Bios (Livelihood), was often termed the "Gate of Hades." In the Whole Sign House system utilized by Valens and Firmicus , the 2nd House does not form a Ptolemaic aspect (conjunction, sextile, square, trine, opposition) to the Ascendant. This state of "aversion" implies that matters of the 2nd House (resources) are disconnected from the immediate volition of the native (Ascendant). This structural weakness explains why malefics in the 2nd House are particularly pernicious—they operate without the "management" or "sight" of the native's life force.2.2 The Joy of the MoonThe Third House, Thea (Goddess), is the place of the Moon's joy. This association with the Moon—the celestial body of flux, travel, family, and the irrational soul—colors all delineations for this house. Planets located here interact with the lunar archetype. For example, Saturn (the planet of structure and coldness) in the Moon's house creates a dissonance that manifests as "coldness with siblings" or mental rigidity, as inferred in the query and supported by the texts.2.3 The Angular FoundationThe Fourth House, Hypogeion (Subterranean), represents the anchor of the chart. It is an angular house, meaning planets here possess high dynamic strength to manifest results. However, because it opposes the Midheaven (the place of visibility), its manifestations are often hidden, relating to the home, the father, ancestry, and the end of life. The presence of luminaries or benefics here suggests a strong foundation that supports the entire life structure, often manifesting most potently in the latter years.2.4 The Doctrine of SectCrucial to Firmicus Maternus and Vettius Valens is the distinction between Day (Diurnal) and Night (Nocturnal) charts. A planet's ability to manifest its significations depends on whether it is "in sect."Diurnal Sect: Sun, Jupiter, Saturn.Nocturnal Sect: Moon, Venus, Mars.

This distinction is vital for interpretations like "Mars in the 2nd House." In a night chart, Mars is the sect malefic and is more constructive (potentially aggressive earning); in a day chart, it is contrary to sect and highly destructive (waste and loss).3. Natal Delineations: Recovering the "Silent" TextsThis section provides the detailed reconstruction of the requested planetary placements, citing specific textual evidence to validate the user's inferences.3.1 The Second House: Bios (Livelihood)The Second House governs movable assets, the substance of life, and the manner of acquisition.3.1.1 Mars in the 2nd HouseInference: "Loss of substance through heat/haste."
Textual Reconstruction: The inference is strongly supported by the classical texts, which view Mars in the 2nd as a primary indicator of financial volatility. Firmicus Maternus, in the Mathesis, outlines that Mars here grants the capacity to earn—often through heat, iron, fire, or strenuous activity—but denies the capacity to retain earnings. The intrinsic nature of Mars is severance; when applied to the house of accumulation, it acts to sever the native from their resources.Rhetorius the Egyptian offers a more severe delineation, particularly when Mars is copresent with Mercury in the 2nd House. He writes that this configuration makes the native "to be exposed" (abandoned) or, if reared, to become a fugitive or exile. The term used is often associated with proscription—the legal confiscation of property. The logic is that Mars maltreats the "substance" of the native's life. In a day chart (out of sect), this manifests as "loss of substance through haste" or foolish expenditure. In a night chart, the loss may be mitigated, or the expenditure may be directed toward strategic ends, but the theme of "heat" consuming "substance" remains constant.Key Insight: The "silence" is effectively broken by the specific connection between Mars and the "Gate of Hades" (2nd House). The lack of aspect to the Ascendant means the native cannot "see" or control the loss; it happens as if by fate or external severance.3.1.2 Sun in the 2nd HouseInference: "Focus on wealth and authority through resources."
Textual Reconstruction: Firmicus Maternus provides a delineation that links the Sun in the 2nd House to the accumulation of wealth through authority and high-status associations. The text states that the Sun here indicates "relative success in financial endeavors" and a "basic generous nature". This generosity is the solar function of radiating light; in the house of money, it manifests as spending or patronage, which in turn generates status.Crucially, Firmicus notes that benefit or recognition comes from "people in authoritative and influential positions". The resources are not merely earned through labor but are granted through prestige or the holding of important positions. This connects to the solar signification of the Father; the placement often implies wealth derived from the father or the patrimony. If the Sun is well-placed (e.g., in Leo or Aries), it promises an increase in substance and "hope or property".Key Insight: The Sun in the 2nd House fuses the native's identity (Sun) with their livelihood (2nd). The inference of "authority through resources" is precise: the native's authority is derived from their command of resources, or their resources are a result of their authority.3.2 The Third House: Thea (Goddess/Brothers)The Third House is the Joy of the Moon. It governs siblings, short journeys, interpretation (dreams/oracles), and religious rites (due to its aspect to the 9th).3.2.1 Saturn in the 3rd HouseInference: "Delays in travel, coldness with siblings."
Textual Reconstruction: While the user notes sources as silent, Firmicus and Rhetorius are quite vocal about Saturn here. Firmicus associates Saturn in the 3rd with "mental discipline" and the "conscientious student". This is because Saturn imposes structure (crystallization) upon the Moon's house (the mind/irrational soul).However, the "coldness" inference is supported by Rhetorius, who notes that Saturn here can cause "unrest and worry" regarding relatives. In a night chart, or if afflicted, it creates alienation from siblings or imposes heavy responsibilities concerning them. Regarding travel, the texts explicitly state that Saturn "tends to limit traveling or confine it to interests concerning the occupation". The travel is not for pleasure (which would be Venus or Jupiter) but for duty.Key Insight: Rhetorius adds a layer of religious or mystical authority to this placement. In a day chart, Saturn in the 3rd can produce "initiates" or "chiefs of the Magi". The "coldness" is thus a form of ascetic detachment necessary for deep study or religious observance.3.2.2 Jupiter in the 3rd HouseInference: "Growth through local environment, beneficial siblings."
Textual Reconstruction: Firmicus states that Jupiter in the 3rd House is especially propitious for "editors, writers, and all those whose profession is concerned with this house". The mechanism is Jupiter's beneficence expanding the topics of communication and networks. It brings "profitable contracts" and "interesting propositions" from the native's circle of acquaintances.The inference of "beneficial siblings" is directly supported. Jupiter here indicates that the native receives help from kin or that the siblings themselves are prosperous. Furthermore, despite the 3rd house ruling short journeys, Jupiter's expansive nature introduces "long-distance nature" to the travel, pushing the boundaries of the local environment. It also affords "mental cheerfulness," protecting the native from the dangers of travel "as if by miracle".3.2.3 Sun in the 3rd HouseInference: "Visibility through communication or siblings."
Textual Reconstruction: Firmicus delineates this placement as favoring a relative "more than the native himself". This is a critical distinction in traditional astrology: planets in the 3rd often transfer their significations to the siblings. The Sun here creates a "distinguished brother" or a sibling who eclipses the native in visibility.For the native, it indicates "success in occupations requiring intellectual work" and "contentment" in the immediate environment. The solar light illuminates the "local," making the native a leader in their neighborhood or immediate social circle. Historical analysis of charts with this placement suggests a trajectory where the native's identity is forged through their communicative abilities or their relationship with a prominent sibling.3.2.4 Venus in the 3rd HouseInference: "Pleasant local environment, grace in speech."
Textual Reconstruction: This is considered one of the most favorable placements for daily life. Firmicus and Volguine note that Venus here "favors relations with the native's associates". It indicates "good education, intellectual tastes, love of learning, and talent for writing".Since the 3rd house is the joy of the Moon, Venus (a nocturnal, moist planet) finds affinity here. Her presence indicates a "pleasant local environment" and "grace in speech," confirming the inference. Rhetorius adds that this can signify an "artistic inspiration whose muse took the form of beautiful women" or beloved sisters. It acts as a mitigation against the harshness of travel, ensuring "congenial conditions" on journeys.3.3 The Fourth House: Hypogeion (Subterranean)The Fourth House represents the home, parents (specifically the father), the earth, and the end of life.3.3.1 Jupiter in the 4th HouseInference: "Expansion of home, land, and stability in old age."
Textual Reconstruction: Firmicus explicitly states that Jupiter in the 4th "favors everything connected with that house". A primary delineation is the promise that "the end of the year [or life] will be better than the beginning". This concept of the "noble ending" is central to 4th house benefics.The placement indicates "family surroundings of a congenial nature" and benefit through parents. It is also a strong indicator of wealth through real estate or "cultivation of lands," as the 4th house rules the earth itself. In the context of the Thema Mundi, where Jupiter is exalted in Cancer (the 4th sign), Jupiter has a natural affinity for the foundation and lineage.3.3.2 Mars in the 4th HouseInference: "Conflict in the home or regarding real estate."
Textual Reconstruction: Firmicus warns of "domestic or family disputes". Mars introduces heat and severance into the home. Traditionally, this signifies "conflict in the home" or regarding real estate and often indicates a "loss of patrimony" or disputes over inheritance.However, if well-placed (e.g., in Scorpio or Capricorn, or in a night chart), it can indicate wealth through "running hotels" or land deals, as Mars provides the energy to manage the "earth". The "sexual bond with mother" mentioned in some modern interpretations  is likely a psychological overlay on the ancient concept of "severance from parents" or the early death of a parent.3.3.3 Sun in the 4th HouseInference: "Honor in the family line or distinguished father."
Textual Reconstruction: Firmicus describes this as "strong attachment to home and family" but also notes "late development" or "reclusive tendencies". This apparent contradiction (Sun = visibility, 4th = hidden) is resolved by the Sun being at the anti-culminating point (midnight). The light is directed inward.This placement signifies "honor in the family line," confirming the user's inference. It favors "all activities involving real estate"  and suggests that the native's greatest success or recognition may come in the latter part of life. It can also indicate "hidden gold" or wealth derived from the earth/mining.3.3.4 Mercury in the 4th HouseInference: "Intellectual home environment, focus on roots."
Textual Reconstruction: Firmicus states that this "intensifies the powers of imagination" and indicates a "strong mind" focused on domestic affairs. It suggests an "intellectual home environment" and a focus on "history or politics".However, Mercury is unstable. Firmicus warns of "differences of opinion in the family". If afflicted, it threatens domestic disputes. The native may be inclined toward "household management" or the administration of the estate. The inference of "focus on roots" is accurate, as Mercury here investigates the "depths" of origin.3.4 The Seventh House: The Setting PlaceThe Seventh House governs marriage, partnerships, and open enemies.3.4.1 Jupiter in the 7th HouseInference: "Growth through partnership, beneficial spouse."
Textual Reconstruction: Firmicus is explicit: "Strong and well aspected, [it] shows conjugal happiness... or success in legal actions". It indicates a "fortunate marriage" and an "outgoing personality".The text notes that benefits come through the partner. The partner may be of a "different race" or from a foreign background (Jupiter ruling foreigners). It also converts enemies into friends, as Jupiter bonifies the house of "open enemies." The inference of "growth through partnership" is fully supported.3.4.2 Mercury in the 7th HouseInference: "Intellectual or communicative partner, focus on contracts."
Textual Reconstruction: Firmicus describes this as indicating a "young, intellectual, or literary partner". The relationship is defined by "considerable communication."However, Mercury can be unstable. Volguine notes it can indicate a spouse who is "too self-centered" or "misunderstandings" if afflicted. It heavily favors "business partnerships" and legal contracts. In a woman's chart, it is traditionally read as an "offer of marriage" or a proposal of a "social or collective nature".3.5 The Twelfth House: Kakos Daimon (Bad Spirit)The Twelfth House is the joy of Saturn and represents hidden things, enemies, and self-undoing.3.5.1 Sun in the 12th HouseInference: "Vitality obscured, hidden struggles."
Textual Reconstruction: The "Bad Spirit" designation is consistent across Valens and Firmicus. Firmicus states that the Sun in the 12th indicates that "one third, if not more, of the native's life will be unfortunate and liable to obscurity".Valens notes that at the time of death, planets often return to the "same difficult configurations" found in the natal 12th house [User Query]. The Sun in the 12th signifies "vitality obscured." The light of the sect is hidden, leading to "hidden struggles," "enemies," or "slavery" (metaphorical or literal).However, Firmicus allows for a "benefic" interpretation if the Sun is well-aspected: it can "illuminate all that is hidden," revealing secrets or enemies. It favors success in "institutional" settings (hospitals, prisons) or "activity behind the scenes".4. Vettius Valens: Specific Zodiacal DelineationsVettius Valens' Anthology is unique in providing delineations that depend not just on the house, but on the specific zoidion (sign) and the planet's condition within it. These interpretations are often fatalistic but reveal the granular detail of the Hellenistic system.5.1 Saturn in the SignsSaturn in Sagittarius: Valens associates this with specific terms. In the chart of Jackie Kennedy, Saturn in Sagittarius in the 2nd House (money/livelihood) was linked to "extended grief" and the "violent death" of a spouse when activated. The logic is that Saturn is in the domicile of Jupiter (benefic) but is contrary to the expansive nature of the sign. Valens notes that "all the terms in Sagittarius indicate varied possibilities," suggesting inherent instability.Saturn in Gemini: In a day chart, this is a dignified Saturn (triplicity ruler). Valens uses this to show intellectual depth or "investigation of sciences," but warns that "the tongue will be impeded from much study".5.2 Mars in the SignsMars in Virgo: Valens gives a vivid example of a native with Mars in Virgo who "expired in bilge water" (drowned/suffocated). In another context, it leads to "industriousness" but also "fault finding" and "anal aggression". Virgo is the sign of Mercury; Mars here is in a rational, dry sign, creating "nervous energy" or "work involving hard materials".Mars in Leo: Valens links this to "betrayal through documents" or "debts" in certain transmissions. Mars in a fixed fire sign indicates stubbornness and visibility. If in the 3rd House, it can indicate "employment" or "breakout roles" (e.g., Armie Hammer example) but also conflict with siblings.5.3 The Luminaries (Sun/Moon) in the SignsSun in Gemini: Valens links this to "being known through speech, communication, or writing". He uses it in calculations for visibility/ascension times. The Sun in a bicorporeal sign often indicates a "double" life or a "complicated" identity.Sun in Pisces: Valens notes this indicates "kingship" but in a watery, mutable way that can lead to "hidden struggles" or "power slipping away".Moon in Virgo: Valens describes this as "industrious" but potentially "critical." In predictive examples, it triggers "convulsions" or "danger of dying" when activated by malefic periods. It combines the irrational soul with the rational mind, leading to "emotional analysis."Moon in Gemini: Valens associates this with "collecting information" or "movement." In a Solar Return or natal context, it indicates "travel and wanderings" and does not provide straight pathways.Moon in Aquarius: Valens describes the Moon here as "malignant" or "haters of their own families" if afflicted, due to the Saturnian rulership. It can indicate "exile" or "injury".5.4 Benefics (Venus/Jupiter) in the SignsVenus in Leo: Valens is surprisingly positive here, noting "mother and nurture," "priesthoods," "gold ring" (status), and "acquisition of property". Venus in the Sun's sign desires visibility and honor, linking affection (Venus) with authority (Sun).Jupiter in Libra: Valens uses this in examples of "fortunate marriage" or success through partners, as Libra is the sign of relationships. However, if squared by malefics, it can indicate "commercial explosion" not necessarily in good taste.5.5 Mercury in the SignsMercury in Cancer: Valens places Mercury here in charts involving "accusations," "poisoning," or "betrayal," especially if averse to the Ascendant. Mercury in a mute, water sign is "dampened," leading to hidden communication or schemes.Mercury in Aquarius: Associated with "exile" or "adultery" in Valens' examples. Mercury in Saturn's sign is intellectually rigid or estranged from the norm.6. In a night chart, Mars in the 2nd may indicate aggressive wealth accumulation; in a day chart, it is almost purely destructive.The Persistence of Joys: The "silence" regarding Saturn in the 3rd is resolved by understanding the 3rd as the Moon's joy.

What is Traditional Astrology?

Traditional astrology refers to techniques developed between the 2nd century BCE and the 17th century CE. It is predictive and event-oriented, prioritizing planetary condition and demonstrable rules.

It evaluates planetary strength through essential dignity, sect, and house rulership. The chart is treated as a map of objective circumstance rather than a psychological profile.

Tradition vs. Modernity

The modern shift in the 20th century emphasized psychology and outer-planet symbolism. Traditional astrology retains the visible planets and classical doctrines of sect, dignity, and house rulership.

Instead of internal archetypes, the focus is on whether a planet can act effectively and what it signifies in external circumstances.

The History of Essential Dignities

Essential dignity is the core scoring system of traditional astrology. It evaluates a planet's ability to act based on its placement.

Developed in the Hellenistic period and refined in medieval Persian and Arabic sources, the system assigns points for rulership, exaltation, triplicity, term, and face. High dignity indicates capacity and authority; low dignity indicates dependence or restraint.

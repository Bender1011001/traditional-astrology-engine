About

A Calculation Engine for Traditional Astrology

Most astrology apps give you a Sun-sign paragraph and call it a reading. This is not that. Traditional Astrology is a rule-based engine that applies classical Hellenistic and medieval techniques to natal charts and produces detailed readings auditable against the calculation.

Why this exists

If you have ever generated a natal chart on a free site, you have seen what most of the industry produces: a few paragraphs about your Sun sign, a Mars-in-Virgo template that has been pasted into a million reports without modification, no timing techniques, no doctrine, no specificity to the actual chart in front of you.

Traditional astrology, as practiced from the Hellenistic period through the 17th century, did not work that way. Vettius Valens in the 2nd century, Abu Ma'shar in the 9th, Guido Bonatti in the 13th, William Lilly in the 17th — they all applied a layered system of essential dignities, sect, mutual receptions, time-lord techniques, and house rulerships to make specific judgments. The rules are knowable, the calculations are deterministic, and the doctrine is preserved in the source texts.

So we built this. The calculation engine is rule-based and grounded in those source texts. Same birth data produces the same chart every time. Every judgment in every report cites the technique it comes from. There is no template substitution and no Sun-sign filler.

What the engine does

The calculation layer uses the Swiss Ephemeris for planetary positions — the same library professional astrology software has used for decades. Houses are computed using the Whole Sign system that all of the Hellenistic and medieval sources actually used, not the Placidus quadrant system that most modern apps default to.

From the calculated positions, the engine computes:

  • Sect (day chart vs. night chart, by Sun's altitude)
  • Essential dignities at all five levels (domicile, exaltation, triplicity, term, face), scored to the points classical sources prescribed
  • Almuten Figuris (Master of Nativity), scored across the five hylegical points using Ibn Ezra's method
  • Mutual receptions by domicile and exaltation
  • Phasis and visibility for each planet (whether it has "voice" in the classical sense)
  • Annual profections with current Lord of the Year
  • Firdaria chains by sect
  • Arabic Lots (Fortune, Spirit, Eros, Necessity, Nemesis, Courage, Victory)
  • Fixed star conjunctions to angles and luminaries (Algol, Regulus, Spica, and others)
  • Humoral temperament from the Ascendant, ruler, Moon, and season

The interpretation layer is where the calculation meets readable language. A large language model is given the structured chart data and a strict rules-of-judgment prompt that enforces traditional doctrine. The model does not invent receptions or aspects — it cites only what the calculation engine has computed. Every reception, every dignity score, every fixed star contact in the report is traceable to the underlying data.

The source texts

The engine's interpretive rules are drawn from primary sources, not modern textbooks:

  • Vettius Valens, Anthology (2nd c.) — for sect, planetary years, time-lord theory
  • Claudius Ptolemy, Tetrabiblos (2nd c.) — for triplicities and primary directions
  • Dorotheus of Sidon, Carmen Astrologicum (1st c.) — for the lot calculations and elections
  • Abu Ma'shar, Great Introduction (9th c.) — for firdaria and chronocrators
  • Ibn Ezra, The Book of Reasons (12th c.) — for the Almuten scoring method
  • Guido Bonatti, Liber Astronomiae (13th c.) — for judgments and elections
  • William Lilly, Christian Astrology (1647) — for horary and dignity scoring

Modern psychological astrology (post-1950s) is deliberately excluded. The engine does not interpret planets as archetypes of inner life. It interprets them as significators of concrete topics, the way the source tradition does.

Limits and disclosures

A few things are worth being honest about.

This is not medical, legal, or financial advice. The reports include traditional humoral correspondences (the four-temperament system used in medieval medicine) as historical material, never as health guidance. They include no investment or legal recommendations.

The reports use AI for the natural-language layer. The calculations are deterministic and verifiable, but the prose that translates the data into readable English comes from a language model. We have written extensive rules into the prompt that prevent the model from inventing aspects, fabricating receptions, or wandering into modern psychological framings — but it is still AI-assisted writing on top of a deterministic chart engine. Some readers care about that distinction; we'd rather state it plainly than pretend otherwise.

The reports are designed to be useful as a starting point for working with traditional astrology. They are not a substitute for a live consultation with a working traditional astrologer if you want personalized human judgment on a specific question. Practitioners like Chris Brennan, Austin Coppock, Demetra George, and others offer that service and we recommend them when appropriate.

If you find a calculation error in a report, contact us and we will investigate and correct it.

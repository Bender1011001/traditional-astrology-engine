What This Calculator Actually Computes

Most birth chart calculators online give you a list of planet positions and stop there. This one goes further.

Enter your birth date, time, and city. The calculator resolves your coordinates from a geocoding database, converts to Julian Day, then runs the Swiss Ephemeris — the same precision library used by professional astrology software — to place all seven classical planets plus the Ascendant and Midheaven. From there it scores essential dignities, determines sect, calculates the five Hermetic Lots, identifies the Almuten Figuris, runs your current annual profection, and locates your active firdaria period.

The output is structured as a traditional reading, not just a table of data. Each placement comes with a dignity score and an interpretation drawn from classical sources.

Essential Dignities — The Part Most Apps Skip

A planet’s sign placement determines its strength. This is not optional — it changes everything.

Traditional astrology uses five levels of essential dignity. A planet in its domicile (home sign) operates at full strength. In exaltation, it works with extra force but sometimes in a lopsided way — exalted planets can be overconfident. Triplicity, term, and face are weaker dignities that still add some support.

The flip side: detriment and fall are the opposite of domicile and exaltation. A planet in detriment is like a guest in an enemy’s house. It can still function, but not easily. Mars in Libra, Venus in Aries, Saturn in Cancer — these are debilitated placements that most modern chart tools mention as curiosities. Traditional astrology treats them as load-bearing facts.

The calculator scores each planet on a numerical scale and flags which planets are working well and which are struggling. That score directly shapes the reading.

Day Chart vs. Night Chart — Why It Matters

Whether you were born while the Sun was above or below the horizon changes which planets are your allies.

This is the doctrine of sect, and it’s one of the first things a traditional astrologer checks. In a day chart — Sun above the horizon — the Sun, Jupiter, and Saturn are the “sect mates” that tend to work constructively. In a night chart, the Moon, Venus, and Mars form the in-sect group.

The practical effect: Mars is significantly more manageable in a night chart than a day chart. A day-chart Mars tends toward rashness and conflict. A night-chart Mars can still be difficult, but it has more self-control. Jupiter and Saturn flip in the opposite direction — Jupiter is more helpful in day charts, Saturn in night charts.

Most apps don’t show you this. Ours identifies your sect, marks which planets are in and out of sect, and factors it into every dignity score.

Whole Sign Houses — Why We Use Them

The house system question is genuinely contentious. Here’s our position and why.

Whole sign houses assign one entire zodiac sign to each house, starting with the rising sign as the first house. If your Ascendant is at 14° Scorpio, all of Scorpio is your first house, all of Sagittarius is your second, and so on. The degree of the Ascendant still matters — it remains the most sensitive point in the chart — but house boundaries fall at 0° of each sign.

The competing systems — Placidus, Regiomontanus, Porphyry — all divide houses based on time arcs or space divisions. They produce “intercepted” signs and wildly unequal house sizes at high latitudes. Someone born near the Arctic Circle in Placidus can have houses that span 90° of the zodiac next to houses that span 10°.

Whole sign houses are the oldest documented system in Western astrology, appearing in Hellenistic texts before any of the medieval systems existed. They’re also the simplest. We use them by default because the source texts we interpret are written around them — it would be incoherent to use Hellenistic techniques in a Placidus framework.

That said, the Ascendant and Midheaven remain the two most important points in the chart regardless of house system. We include both.

Your Profection Year — What’s Active Right Now

Annual profections are one of the most practical timing tools in traditional astrology.

Each year of life corresponds to a different house, cycling through all twelve. Age 0 = first house. Age 1 = second house. Age 12 brings you back to the first. Age 25 = second house again. The house activated in your current profection year becomes the dominant theme — and the planet that rules that sign becomes the “Lord of the Year.”

If your 10th house (career) is activated and the Lord of the Year is Jupiter in good condition, expect the year to carry professional momentum. If the Lord of the Year is Saturn combust the Sun and in detriment, that same year is likely to feel like grinding through resistance in career matters.

This technique takes about 30 seconds to apply once you know the chart. The calculator runs it automatically and shows you which house is active, who the Lord of the Year is, and what condition that planet is in.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Do I need my exact birth time?
    The more precise the better. Birth time determines the Ascendant and house positions. An error of even 4 minutes shifts the Ascendant degree, and at sign boundaries it can change your rising sign entirely. Hospital birth certificates are usually accurate to the minute. If you only have an approximate time, check the “I don’t know my birth time” box — the calculator will produce a solar chart instead.
  • What’s the difference between traditional and modern astrology?
    Modern astrology (post-1900) centers on the outer planets — Uranus, Neptune, Pluto — and psychological interpretation. Traditional astrology uses only the seven classical planets visible to the naked eye, emphasizes essential dignity and sect, and focuses on concrete life events rather than personality archetypes. The two traditions ask different questions and use different tools.
  • Why doesn’t the calculator show Uranus, Neptune, or Pluto?
    Traditional technique was developed before their discovery and doesn’t have a role for them. Including them in a traditional reading would be anachronistic — like citing Newton in a pre-Newtonian physics text. If you want them, there are plenty of modern chart calculators that include them.
  • What is the Lot of Fortune?
    One of five Hermetic Lots, it’s a calculated point (not a planet) that shifts based on whether you have a day or night chart. In day charts: Ascendant + Moon − Sun. In night charts: Ascendant + Sun − Moon. The Lot of Fortune marks where your material circumstances and physical world tend to manifest. It often falls in a surprising house.

Tool Comparison

Astrolabe pioneered automated astrology reports in the 1980s. Traditional Astrology is built for the same core purpose — quality automated natal chart reports — but with a subscription model designed for practitioners generating readings at volume, not consumers ordering one-offs. This comparison is honest and factual. The right tool depends entirely on how you work.

01 Two Different Models

Astrolabe (alabe.com) built its business on per-report sales: you pay for each report individually, and that model has served casual users and occasional practitioners well since the 1980s. Their legacy software and reputation are genuine. If you need one report every few months, their per-report pricing works fine.

Traditional Astrology is built around a different assumption: that serious astrology practitioners generate readings regularly, and the cost model should reflect that. A flat monthly subscription with unlimited report generation changes the economics entirely when you have consistent client volume. You stop doing the per-report math and start focusing on the work.

Neither model is universally better. The decision hinges on one question: how many reports do you generate per month?

04 Technique Comparison

Astrolabe's reports blend modern and traditional astrology, with a primary emphasis on psychological interpretation of planetary placements. Their framework reflects the mainstream Western approach of the late 20th century — planet-sign-house combinations with psychological overtones, incorporating outer planets (Uranus, Neptune, Pluto).

Traditional Astrology uses strictly classical pre-1700s techniques derived from ancient and medieval sources. Every judgment is grounded in the following framework:

  • Essential dignities — domicile, exaltation, triplicity, term, and face rulerships for every planet
  • Sect — day/night distinction that governs how planets function in a chart
  • Arabic lots — Lot of Fortune, Lot of Spirit, and derivative lots
  • House rulers — the chain of planetary testimony across houses
  • Annual profections — a yearly timing method identifying the Lord of the Year
  • Firdaria planetary periods — long-range timing by sect sequence

Neither approach is superior in an absolute sense. Psychological astrology and classical predictive astrology serve genuinely different purposes. If your clients want insight into their psychological landscape and personal growth themes, Astrolabe-style reports fit that. If your clients want traditional predictive astrology grounded in classical methods — concrete timing, dignified planets, lot-based analysis — Traditional Astrology's framework is built for that work.

05 Bulk Report Generation

This is the clearest operational differentiator between the two tools.

Astrolabe has no bulk upload feature. Every report is generated individually: you enter one client's birth data, purchase, and receive one report. If you have 20 clients in a week, you repeat that process 20 times. For Etsy sellers or practitioners with consistent client volume, this is a significant time cost.

Traditional Astrology's Practitioner plan includes a CSV-to-ZIP batch tool. The workflow is:

  • Download the CSV template from your dashboard
  • Fill in client rows: name, birth date, birth time, birth city, country
  • Upload the CSV — Traditional Astrology calculates each chart and generates a labeled PDF for every client
  • Download a single ZIP file with all PDFs named by client

For a batch of 20 clients, Astrolabe means 20 individual orders and 20 separate downloads. Traditional Astrology means one upload and one download. The time difference at moderate volume is measured in hours per month, not minutes.

06 User Interface

Astrolabe's interface reflects its origins in the 1990s and early 2000s. It is functional — the underlying calculations are sound — but it was not designed for modern web workflows. Navigation is form-based, the visual design is dated, and there is no concept of a persistent dashboard, saved chart library, or client management.

Traditional Astrology is built as a contemporary web application. The interface uses a dark theme optimized for extended use, a responsive layout that works on mobile and desktop, saved chart storage with client labels, chart search and filtering, and a unified dashboard for accessing batch tools and past reports. If the quality and efficiency of your working environment matters to how much you get done, this is a meaningful difference in daily use.

07 Horary Astrology

Astrolabe does not offer a horary tool. Their product line is focused on natal chart reports.

Traditional Astrology includes a free classical horary calculator. When a question is put to it, the tool:

  • Erects a chart for the current moment and location
  • Identifies the significators for the querent and quesited by house and sign
  • Checks five classical perfectional conditions: applying aspect, translation of light, collection of light, prohibition, and mutual reception
  • Returns a scored YES/NO verdict with the full reasoning displayed — which conditions fired, which blocked, and why

For practitioners who use horary as part of their client work — whether as a standalone service or as a complement to natal readings — this is a tool that stands on its own without any subscription requirement. It is available at traditional-astrology.com/horary.html.

08 Who Each Tool Is For

Rather than framing this as a winner, here is an honest account of which situation favors each product.

Choose Astrolabe if…

  • You occasionally need a one-off report for a personal client and volume is low (1–2 per month)
  • You prefer modern psychological interpretation of planetary placements
  • You already use their desktop report-writer software and it fits your workflow
  • You want a product with decades of established reputation

Choose Traditional Astrology if…

  • You generate more than 2–3 reports per month and want the cost to stop scaling with volume
  • You sell astrology readings on Etsy or run a practice with regular client throughput
  • You want classical traditional techniques grounded in pre-1700s methods
  • You want a bulk CSV upload tool to generate a batch of PDFs in one operation
  • You want horary as part of your practice toolkit
  • You want a modern interface with saved charts, client labels, and a dashboard

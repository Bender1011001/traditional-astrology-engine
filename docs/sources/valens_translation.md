# BOOK I

## I.1 — Περὶ τῆς τῶν ἀστέρων φύσεως (The nature of the stars), pp. 1–5

Kroll heads the book **"Desunt plura"** — *much is missing*. The work as transmitted begins mid-sentence; there is no surviving introduction.

### The Sun **[E]**
> The all-seeing Sun, being fiery and a light of the intellect, an organ of psychic perception, signifies in a nativity: kingship, leadership, mind, practical wisdom, form, motion, height of fortune, the transaction of the gods, judgment, publicity, action, leadership of crowds, **father**, master, friendship, honoured persons, honours of images and statues and crowns, high-priesthoods of the fatherland.
>
> Of the body's parts he rules the head, the sense-organs, the **right eye**, the sides, the heart, the pneumatic or sensitive motion, the sinews. Of substances, gold; of crops, wheat and barley. **He is of the diurnal sect; in colour yellowish-brown, in taste sharp.**

### The Moon **[E]**
> The Moon, having come to be from the reflection of the solar light and possessing a **bastard light** (νόθον φῶς), signifies for men at birth: life, body, **mother**, conception, form, face, sight, cohabitation or lawful marriage, nourishment, elder brother, housekeeping, queen, mistress, money, fortune, city, gathering of crowds, receipts, expenditures, household, ships, sojourning abroad, **wanderings — for she does not provide straight paths, on account of Cancer.**
>
> Of the body's parts she rules the **left eye**, the stomach, the breasts, the genitals, the spleen, the meninges, the marrow — whence she produces dropsical conditions. Of substances, silver and glass. **She is of the nocturnal sect; in colour green, in taste salty.**

### Saturn **[E]**
> Saturn makes those born under him petty, malicious, full of cares, self-abasing, solitary, sullen, concealing their guile, austere, downcast, **having a hypocritical gaze**, squalid, **black-clothed**, importunate, gloomy-faced, suffering ill.
>
> He makes also humiliations, sluggishness, inactivity, obstructions of what is undertaken, long-lasting lawsuits, overturnings of affairs, concealments, confinements, bonds, griefs, accusations, tears, **orphanhoods**, captivities, exposures of infants; and farmers, **on account of his ruling the earth**, and renters of properties and tax-collectors.
>
> Of substances he rules lead, wood and stones. Of the body's limbs: legs, knees, sinews, lymph, phlegm, bladder, kidneys, and the inward hidden parts. He is indicative of such injuries as **arise from cold and moisture** — dropsy, pains of the sinews, gout, cough, dysentery, ruptures, spasms. He makes also unmarried men, widowhoods, orphanhoods, childlessness. **He is the star of Nemesis, and of the diurnal sect; in colour castor-like, in taste astringent.**

### Jupiter **[E]**
> Jupiter signifies childbearing, offspring, desire, loves, associations, acquaintanceships, **friendships of great men**, prosperity, salaries, great gifts, abundance of crops, justice, offices, citizenships, reputations, presidencies of temples, arbitration of judgments, trusts, **inheritances**, brotherhood, partnership, **adoption**, confirmation of good things, deliverance from evils, **release from bonds**, freedom, deposits, money, stewardships.
>
> Of the body's limbs he rules the inner thighs and the feet — whence he furnishes running in athletic contests — and inwardly the seed, the womb, the liver, the right-hand parts. Of substances, tin. **He is of the diurnal sect; in colour grey and rather white, in taste sweet.**

### Mars **[E]**
> Mars signifies violences, wars, plunderings, outcries, insults, adulteries, removals of possessions, expulsions, exiles, estrangements of parents, captivities, corruptions of women, embryotomies, intimacies, marriages, removals of goods, falsehoods, empty hopes, violent thefts, robberies, severings of friends, anger, fighting, abuse, enmities, lawsuits. He brings violent murders, cuttings and bloodlettings, attacks of fevers, ulcerations, eruptions, burnings, bonds, tortures, perjury, wandering.
>
> He makes also military commands and polemarchs, hoplites, leaderships, huntings and chases, **falls from a height or from four-footed beasts**.
>
> Of the body's parts: head, seat, private parts; inwardly the blood, the seminal passages, the bile, the excretion, the hinder parts. **He has also the hard and the abrupt.** Of substances, iron; and the adornment of garments **on account of Aries**; wine and pulses. **He is of the nocturnal sect; in colour red, in taste bitter.**

### Venus **[E]**
> Venus is desire and love, and signifies **mother** and nourishment. She makes priesthoods, gymnasiarchies, wearing of gold, wearing of crowns, gladnesses, friendships, companionships, purchases of ornament, transactions toward the good, marriages, clean arts, fine voices, music-making, sweet melodies, comeliness, painting, **mixtures and varieties of colours**, purple-dyeing and perfumery, and the masters of these; work in emerald, stone and ivory; gold-buying, gold-adorning, barbers, lovers of cleanliness. Market-inspectorships, measures, weights, trade, workshops, givings, takings, laughter, cheerfulness, ornament, hunting from wet places. Benefits and exceptional reputations **from royal or household women**.
>
> Of the body's limbs: neck, face, lips, smell, and the **front parts from foot to head**; the organs of intercourse; inwardly the lung. Of substances, precious stones and varied ornament; of crops, the olive. **She is of the nocturnal sect; in colour white, in taste most oily.**

### Mercury **[E]**
> Mercury signifies education, letters, examination, reason, brotherhood, interpretation, heraldry, numbers, reckoning, geometry, commerce, youth, games, theft, community, message, service, gain, discovery, following, athletics, wrestling, voice-training, sealing, sending letters, weighing, testing money, hearing, being various.
>
> **He is giver both of understanding and of practical wisdom, lord of brothers and of younger children**, and maker of every market and banking art. Properly he makes temple-builders, modellers, statue-makers, doctors, grammarians, lawyers, orators, philosophers, architects, musicians, diviners, sacrificers, bird-watchers, **dream-interpreters**, plaiters, weavers.
>
> Of the body's parts: hands, shoulders, fingers, joints, belly, hearing, artery, intestines, tongue.

### ⭐ The conditional rule — benefic and malefic, 5,~5 **[G]**

> Οἱ μὲν οὖν **ἀγαθοποιοὶ** ἐπιτόπως καὶ καλῶς κείμενοι τὰ ἴδια ἀποτελοῦσι κατά τε τὴν ἰδίαν καὶ τὴν τοῦ ζῳδίου φύσιν, συνεπικεχραμένης τῆς ἑκάστου ἀστέρος μαρτυρίας ἢ συμπαρουσίας. **παραπεπτωκότες δὲ ἐναντιωμάτων εἰσὶ δηλωτικοί**· ὁμοίως δὲ καὶ οἱ **κακοποιοὶ** χρηματίζοντες ἐπιτόπως καὶ τῆς αἱρέσεως **ἀγαθῶν δοτῆρες καὶ μειζόνων τάξεων καὶ προκοπῶν δηλωτικοί**, ἀχρημάτιστοι δὲ ἐκπτώσεις καὶ καταιτιασμοὺς ἀποτελοῦσι.

> The **benefics**, well placed and in their proper places, accomplish their own effects according to their own nature and the nature of the sign, the testimony or co-presence of each star being mixed in. **But when fallen, they are indicative of oppositions.** Likewise also the **malefics**: when transacting in their proper places and of the sect, they are **givers of good things, and indicative of greater rank and of advancement**; but when not transacting, they accomplish falls and accusations.

### The configuration vocabulary and each planet's domain, 5,~13 **[G]**

> Ἕκαστος δὲ ἀστὴρ ἰδίας ἐστὶ κύριος πρὸς τὸν κόσμον οὐσίας πρός τε **συμπάθειαν καὶ ἀντιπάθειαν καὶ ἀλληλοπάθειαν** … κατὰ **συναφὴν** καὶ **ἀπόρροιαν** καὶ **καθυπερτέρησιν** ἢ καὶ **ἐμπερίσχεσιν** καὶ **δορυφορίαν** καὶ **ἀκτινοβολίαν** καὶ προσαγορίαν τῶν δεσποτῶν. Σελήνη μὲν **προνοίας**, Ἥλιος δὲ **αὐγῆς**, **ἀγνοίας δὲ καὶ ἀνάγκης Κρόνος**, δόξης δὲ καὶ στεμμάτων καὶ προθυμίας **Ζεὺς** κύριος καθέστηκε, **πράξεως δὲ καὶ μόχθου ὁ τοῦ Ἄρεως**, ἔρωτος δὲ καὶ ἐπιθυμίας καὶ κάλλους **ὁ τῆς Ἀφροδίτης**, **νόμου δὲ καὶ συνηθείας καὶ πίστεως ὁ τοῦ Ἑρμοῦ**.

> Each star is lord of its own substance in relation to the cosmos, with respect to **sympathy and antipathy and mutual affection** … by **application** and **separation**, and **overcoming**, or also **enclosure** and **spear-bearing** and **hurling of rays**, and the addressing of the lords. The **Moon** is lord of **forethought**; the **Sun** of **radiance**; **Saturn** of **ignorance and necessity**; **Jupiter** of reputation and crowns and eagerness; **Mars** of **action and toil**; **Venus** of love and desire and beauty; **Mercury** of **law and custom and trust**.

## I.2 — Περὶ τῆς τῶν ιβ′ ζῳδίων φύσεως (The twelve signs), pp. 5–14

**[E]** for all sign entries below; the attribute lists were transcribed, the chorography and paranatellonta were read but not preserved.

**Aries** — house of Mars; masculine, tropical, terrestrial, commanding, fiery, free, upward-tending, half-voiced, good, changeable, administrative, public, political, few-offspring, **"orderly midheaven and cause of reputation"**, disjunct, eclipsic. *Natives: brilliant, notable, commanding, just, hating wickedness, free, bold in judgment, boastful, great-souled, unstable, irregular, high-necked, threatening.*

**Taurus** — feminine, solid, lying in its own turning, backward-facing, rising from the hinder parts.

**Gemini** — house of Mercury; masculine, two-bodied, well-voiced, upward-tending, airy, **feminized**, slave-free, barren, public. *Natives: lovers of learning, practising letters and education, poetic, music-loving, voice-trainers, quick-witted, undertaking trusts; also mixed, commercial, meddlesome, **initiates of hidden things**.*

**Cancer** — house of the Moon; feminine, tropical, **ὡροσκόπος κόσμου — Ascendant of the cosmos**, servile, downward-tending, voiceless, watery, good, changeable, public, crowd-related, political, many-offspring, amphibious. *Natives: reputation-loving, theatrical, pleasure-loving, fond of company; **unstable in judgment, saying one thing and thinking another**; not remaining in one occupation or at most two; given to wanderings and sojourns abroad.*

**Leo** — house of the Sun; masculine, free, fiery, extreme, intellectual, **kingly**, seated, good, upward-tending, changeable, solid, commanding, political, arctic, prone to anger. *Natives: notable, good, unchanging, just, hating wickedness, insubordinate, **hating flattery**, beneficent.* **"And if the ruler happens to be angular, or with benefics, they become brilliant, renowned, tyrannical, kingly."**

**Virgo** — house of Mercury; feminine, winged, human-formed, delicate, **"in figure the form of Justice (Δίκη)"**, two-bodied, barren, slave-free, sterile, downward-tending, earthy, common, half-voiced and voiceless, bodily, incomplete, changeable, workshop-related, twofold. *Natives: good, simple, mystical, full of cares, passing life variously, managers of others' property, trustworthy, good stewards, **secretaries advanced from words or reckonings**, hypocritical, blameworthy, initiates of hidden things; **spending in their first years, more prosperous in their middle years**.*

**Libra** — house of Venus; masculine, tropical, human-formed, upward-tending, earthy, feminized, voiced, good, changeable, **diminishing of possessions**, **ὑπόγειον κόσμου — IC of the cosmos**, public, eclipsic, presiding over crops, of wine, of oil, of crowns, of perfumes, workshop-related. *Natives: good and just **but envious**, desirers of others' goods, moderate; **losing what they first acquired and coming to later gains**; living irregularly; presiding over measures or weights or plenty.*

**Scorpio** — house of Mars; feminine, solid, watery, many-seeded, corrupting, downward-tending, voiceless, servile, unchanging, cause of foul odour, **taking away possessions**, eclipsic, many-offspring. *Natives: guileful, wicked, rapacious, murderous, traitors, unchanging, secret plotters, thieves.*

**Sagittarius** — house of Jupiter; masculine, fiery, upward-tending, voiced, **"moist because of Argo"**, good, winged, changeable, two-bodied, twofold, **enigmatic**, few-offspring, half-complete, commanding, kingly. *Natives: good, just, great-souled, critical, generous, brother-loving, friend-loving; **diminishing what they first acquired, then acquiring again**; overcoming enemies; reputation-loving, beneficent, notable, **weaving matters enigmatically**.*

**Capricorn** — house of Saturn; feminine, tropical, earthy, corrupting, sterile, downward-tending, chilled, voiceless, servile, **cause of evils**, licentious, ministering, unchanging, twofold, self-moist, half-complete, hump-shaped, **lame**, **δύσις κόσμου — Descendant of the cosmos**, indicative of toils and labours, stone-cutting, agricultural. *Natives: bad, **"good and simple by hypocrisy"**, laborious, full of cares, sleepless, laughter-loving, plotters of great works, liars, blameworthy, shameful.*

**Aquarius** — masculine, solid, human-shaped, silver-loving, single-born, sterile, very cold, free, upward-tending, feminized, unchanging, **bad**, few-offspring, cause of toils through injustice or of burdens and hard labour, industrious, public. *Natives: envious, hating their own, unchanging, single-minded, sullen, guileful, concealing themselves in wickedness, misanthropic, godless, accusers, traitors.*

**Pisces** — read, delineation not preserved. **[S]**

### ⭐ The four Thema Mundi angles, distributed through the chapter
Cancer = **ὡροσκόπος κόσμου** (Ascendant) · Libra = **ὑπόγειον κόσμου** (IC) · Capricorn = **δύσις κόσμου** (Descendant) · Aries = **κόσμιον μεσουράνημα** (MC). Internally consistent by whole sign from Cancer, and matching Firmicus's *Thema Mundi* (*Mathesis* III.1).

**⭐ The sentence that closes I.3 (printed p. 19,4–7) [G]** — *Ἐκθέμενοι οὖν διδασκαλικῶς περὶ ἑκάστης μόνης μοίρας τί ἀποτελεῖ, τοῦ οἰκοδεσπότου αὐτοῖς ἐπικειμένου ἀποτελέσει τὸ ἴδιον ἤτοι φαῦλον ἢ ἀγαθόν.* — *"Having set out **for teaching purposes** what each degree **by itself** accomplishes: with **the domicile-lord lying upon them**, it will accomplish its own — **either base or good**."*

This governs the whole table. A bound delineation is the degree's own contribution stated in isolation; the condition of the overlying domicile lord decides whether it comes out base or good. It is never a free-standing verdict. Carried into the engine as `BOUND_QUALIFIER`.

## I.5–I.24 — summary entries

**I.6 [E]** — *To find the Midheaven by hand: taking from the setting degree, according to the ascensions of the clima, up to the opposite point, release the half of these from the western degree; wherever it falls, that is the Midheaven.*

**I.7 [E]** — *Since Aries ascends in 20, Libra ascends in 40, to the completion of 60. For however much each sign ascends in, **the diametrically opposite sign takes up the remainder to 60**.*

**I.8 [E]** — *Concerning hearing and seeing signs, it is to be known **from the ascensions**. Pisces sees Taurus; for from Pisces the ascension in the 2nd clima is 160, and from Taurus 200 … and the ascension of the two together approaches 360.*

**I.10 [E]** — *The order of the stars according to the day is: **Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn**.* Reckoned by subtracting sevens from the years since **Augustus** plus the days from **Thoth** to the birthday.

**I.11 [E]** — *The complete years of Augustus 148 and intercalary 36 and of Thoth 1 make 185; from these I subtract 26 sevens; 3 remain. These from the Sun — **the year terminates at Mars**.* And: *But **that those born in the same year should have obtained one and the same rulership does not seem to have reason**. … the ancients grasped it from the new moon of Thoth, but **more naturally from the rising of the Dog Star**.*
⚠️ **This "Lord of the Year" is calendrical, NOT the profection of IV.11.**

**I.12 [E]** — *Of the **masculine** signs the first 2½ degrees are masculine, the next 2½ feminine; of the **feminine** signs the first 2½ feminine, the next masculine, the next feminine.*

**I.18 [E]** — *From Leo to Libra the Moon descends northerly; from Scorpio to Capricorn descends southerly; from Aquarius to Aries ascends southerly; from Taurus to Cancer ascends northerly.*

**I.19** — titled **Ἱππάρχειον**, "the Hipparchan method." **[S]**

**I.20 [S]** — regnal-year tables from Augustus. Emperor list runs to **Gordian and Philip (244–249)**, seventy years after Valens's death; Kroll flags the seam. Evidence of later editing.

### I.21 — Περὶ συμπαρουσίας καὶ συγκράσεως (Co-presence and mixture), pp. 37–41 **[E]**
- **Saturn + Jupiter** — *sympathetic*: benefits **from the dead**, adoptions, lords of properties, guardians, managers of others' affairs, economical, lovers of learning.
- **Saturn + Mars** — ***enemies***, producers of oppositions and demolitions: factions among relatives, ill-will, deceits, plots, judgments — ***except if, falling in their own or in busy signs and witnessed by benefics, they construct notable and brilliant nativities***; though uncertain as to happiness, with inglorious dangers or betrayals.

> That we may make the demonstration briefly: **whatever figure the stars are found making toward one another AT THE NATIVITY — whether good or base, according to the FOUNDATION of the casting — that is what they will accomplish when they are lords of the times.**

Third independent statement of the same constraint (with IV.16, p.184 and IV.5, p.163). **A time-lord delivers what the natal figure already contains; it does not introduce anything new.** Three statements across three books is as close to emphasis as Valens gets, and it is the doctrinal ground for engine rule `valens_hypostasis_caps_timing`.

## ⭐ VII.2, p. 267 — a finer division, and nothing arrives unmixed **[G]**

> Τῶν μὲν οὖν καθολικῶν χρόνων κατὰ σύγκρισιν προκειμένων **λεπτομερεστέραν διαίρεσιν ἐκ πείρας καὶ πόνου ἐξηρευνημένην** ὑποτάξω. πρόλογον δὲ πᾶσι τοῖς τὰ βέλτιστα μετιέναι βουλομένοις … ἢ καὶ τοῖς **πολυχρονίαν νόσον τοῦ μαθήματος ἀνίατον κτησαμένοις** νηπτικῶς περὶ τὴν ἀγωγὴν φέρεσθαι καὶ μὴ παρέργως μηδεμιᾷ αἱρέσει χρήσασθαι.

> Of the universal times set out by comparison, I shall add **a more minute division, sought out from experience and toil**. As a preface to all who wish to pursue what is best — and especially to those who have contracted **the long and incurable disease of this study** — [I urge them] to approach the method **soberly**, and not to use any method carelessly.

> κἂν μὲν **εὔτονος** ᾖ τῶν ἀγαθοποιῶν χρονογραφία κατὰ τὴν γένεσιν τύχῃ, **πρᾶξις ἢ δόξα γενήσεται ΜΕΘ' ΕΝΑΝΤΙΩΜΑΤΩΝ ΚΑΙ ΑΝΑΛΩΜΑΤΩΝ**· ἐὰν δὲ ἡ τῶν κακοποιῶν, ἀπραξίαι καὶ ζημίαι … **μέχρις ἂν ἡ τῶν ἀγαθοποιῶν παραιτήσῃ.**

> And if the chronography **of the benefics** falls **strong** at the nativity, action or reputation will come about — ***with oppositions and expenditures***; but if that **of the malefics**, ineffectivenesses and penalties … **until that of the benefics intercedes.**

**Even a strong benefic period arrives "with oppositions and expenditures".** Nothing in this system is unmixed, and a malefic stretch runs *until the benefics intercede* rather than until it exhausts itself. Worth carrying into any prose that reports a favourable period.

## ⭐ VII.2, pp. 270–271 — why several time-lords act at once, and the Cleanthes quotation **[G]**

> **Ὅτι δὲ οἱ πλείους τῶν ἀστέρων ἢ καὶ πάντες κατὰ τὸν αὐτὸν χρόνον δύνανται ἐνεργεῖν** πρὸς τὰ ἀποτελέσματα, ἐκ τούτων ἔστι συνιδεῖν. εὑρίσκομεν ἐν τῷ κοσμικῷ καταστήματι τῆς γῆς **κατὰ πᾶσαν ἡμέραν καὶ πᾶσαν ὥραν** τρεψίματα γινόμενα, οἷον γένους θανάτους κληρονομίας δόξας καθαιρέσεις σίνη πάθη καὶ τὰ λοιπά, **ὅσα τῷ τῶν ἀνθρώπων βίῳ συνέπεται ἀγαθά τε καὶ φαῦλα.**

> **That most of the stars, or even all, can act toward the outcomes at the same time** is to be seen from this. We find in the cosmic condition of the earth, **on every day and every hour**, changes coming about — births, deaths, inheritances, reputations, demolitions, injuries, afflictions, and the rest, **both good and base, that attend the life of men.**

His argument for simultaneous time-lords is empirical: everything happens to someone every hour, so a scheme in which one planet rules at a time cannot account for the world.

Then, at the climax of the argument on fate, he quotes **Cleanthes** (Kroll's apparatus: *Cleanth. fr. 527*):

> **ἄγου δέ μ' ὦ Ζεῦ καὶ σύ γ' ἡ πεπρωμένη,**
> **ὅποι ποθ' ὑμῖν εἰμι διατεταγμένος·**
> **ὡς ἕψομαί γ' ἄοκνος. ἢν δὲ μὴ θέλω,**
> **κακὸς γενόμενος αὐτὸ τοῦτο πείσομαι.**

> ***Lead me, O Zeus, and thou, Destiny,***
> ***wherever I have been appointed by you;***
> ***for I shall follow unhesitating. And if I should not wish to,***
> ***having become base, I shall suffer this very thing all the same.***

Valens's frame is **Stoic**, and he says so by quotation rather than argument. Useful context for how the tradition understood what it was doing: not prediction as leverage over fate, but foreknowledge of what will happen either way.

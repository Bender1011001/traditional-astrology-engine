The Panaretos Protocol: A Technical and Historical Reconstruction of the Hermetic Planetary Lots
1. Executive Summary: The Mathematical Architecture of Hellenistic Fate
The study of Hellenistic astrology is frequently obscured by the erosion of textual transmission, yet the recovery of the "Lots" (kleroi)—mathematical points derived from planetary vectors—remains one of the most significant technical achievements of the 20th and 21st-century philological restoration. Among these, the system recorded by the 4th-century astrologer Paulus Alexandrinus in his Eisagogika (Introduction to Astrology) stands as the rigorous apex of the "Hermetic" tradition. Unlike the earlier, more fluid traditions recorded by Vettius Valens, Paulus presents a crystallized, symmetrical system of seven lots explicitly attributed to the text Panaretos, a lost work ascribed to the legendary Hermes Trismegistus.
This research report provides an exhaustive technical specification for the primary Hermetic Lots beyond the luminaries—specifically the Lots of Necessity, Eros, Courage, Victory, and Nemesis. It is designed to serve as a definitive reference for the historian of science and the technical implementer, stripping away centuries of medieval accretion to restore the precise algorithmic definitions of the Alexandrian school. We analyze the philological basis for the formulas, the philosophical rationale for their vector directions, and the precise arithmetic required to calculate them in a modern computational environment.
The investigation reveals a profound geometric logic governing the Panaretos system: a strict bifurcation of the cosmos into realms of "Will" (Daimon/Spirit) and "Circumstance" (Tyche/Fortune). The benefic planets (Venus, Jupiter) are mathematically anchored to the Lot of Spirit, extending the native’s volitional capacity, while the malefic planets (Mars, Saturn) and the contentious Mercury are anchored to the Lot of Fortune, representing the external constraints and "subterranean fates" that impinge upon the physical life. This report synthesizes these insights into a unified theory of Hermetic calculation, providing the necessary implementation specifications to reconstruct the ancient "Web of Fate."

2. Historical Foundations: The Panaretos and the Alexandrian Context
2.1 The Source Material: Paulus and the Hermetic Lineage
The primary authority for the formulas under investigation is Chapter 23 of Paulus’s Introduction. Writing in 378 CE, Paulus occupies a critical juncture in the history of astrology. He writes after the foundational period of the 1st and 2nd centuries (represented by Dorotheus, Ptolemy, and Valens) but before the complete collapse of the Western Roman philosophical tradition. His work is characterized by a "back-to-basics" rigidity, often citing "the ancients" to distinguish his methods from the varying practices of his contemporaries.1
Paulus explicitly cites the Panaretos ("All-Virtuous") as the source for his lot calculations. The commentary by the 6th-century Neo-Platonist Olympiodorus the Younger, delivered as lectures in Alexandria, confirms this text was attributed to Hermes Trismegistus.1 This attribution is not merely ornamental; it signals adherence to the "Egyptian" stream of astrology, which prioritized the use of Lots as distinct "birth deities" capable of describing specific fated events, in contrast to the rationalizing "scientific" approach of Claudius Ptolemy, who attempted to minimize the use of lots (retaining only Fortune) to align astrology with Aristotelian physics.2
The Panaretos system, as preserved by Paulus, represents a specific "Hermetic recension." While Vettius Valens (c. 175 CE) records a diversity of lot formulas—often calculating Eros and Necessity purely from the arc between Spirit and Fortune—Paulus presents a system where every planet has a dedicated lot.1 This shift from a purely luminary-based model (Valens) to a fully planetary model (Paulus) marks a significant evolution in technical complexity, embedding the specific nature of each planet (e.g., Venus’s binding power, Saturn’s exclusionary coldness) into the mathematical extraction of the lot.
2.2 The "Four Birth Deities" and the Neoplatonic Integration
The cultural weight of these calculations is underscored by their association with the "Four Birth Deities" mentioned by the Roman writer Macrobius in his Saturnalia. Macrobius identifies four powers that preside over a human birth: Daimon (Spirit), Tyche (Fortune), Eros (Love), and Ananke (Necessity).4
This quarternary is not arbitrary. It maps the human experience onto two axes:
1. The Axis of Agency (Sun/Spirit): Representing the intellect, the soul, and what is done by choice.
2. The Axis of Consequence (Moon/Fortune): Representing the body, the material conditions, and what is suffered or experienced.
In the Panaretos system, the Lots of Eros and Victory are derivatives of the Axis of Agency, while Necessity, Courage, and Nemesis are derivatives of the Axis of Consequence. This philosophical structure dictates the mathematics: to calculate a lot related to the body or fate (like Nemesis), one must anchor the calculation in the Lot of Fortune. To calculate a lot related to intent or association (like Victory), one must anchor it in the Lot of Spirit. The formulas are thus mathematical expressions of a theological argument regarding the nature of fate and free will.1
2.3 The Divergence from Valens
A critical task for the historian is distinguishing the Paulus/Hermetic formulas from those found in Vettius Valens’ Anthology. This distinction is frequently a source of confusion in modern literature and software implementation.
* The Valens Tradition (2nd Century): Often defines the Lot of Eros as the arc from Spirit to Fortune (or vice versa), and Necessity as the arc from Fortune to Spirit. These are "Lots of the Lots," derived solely from the relationship between the two primary abstract points without referencing the position of Venus or Mercury.4
* The Paulus Tradition (4th Century): Defines the Lot of Eros using the coordinate of Venus, and the Lot of Necessity using the coordinate of Mercury.
The Paulus tradition is the subject of this report. It is considered the "High Hermetic" standard because it integrates the planetary signifiers directly. As noted in recent scholarship 6, modern implementations (such as those in major astrological software engines) increasingly default to the Paulus formulas for Eros and Necessity because they provide a more granular, planet-specific analysis that aligns with the "Seven Hermetic Lots" schema.

4. The Five Planetary Hermetic Lots: Detailed Analysis
We now proceed to the core of the request: the five lots associated with the star-planets (Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn). In the Panaretos tradition, these lots are derived not from planet-to-planet arcs (like the Lot of the Father = Sun to Saturn), but from Planet-to-Lot or Lot-to-Planet arcs.
This distinction is crucial. It suggests that these lots represent the intersection of a planetary principle with the fundamental domains of Fate (Fortune) or Will (Spirit).
4.1 The Lot of Necessity (Ananke) — Mercury
Greek Name: Ananke (Necessity, Constraint, Compulsion).
Associated Planet: Mercury (Hermes).
Anchor: Lot of Fortune.
Concept and Rationale: Mercury is the planet of contest, communication, exchange, and argumentation. In the Hermetic schema, it is associated with Ananke—the inescapable constraints of fate, struggle, and the exigencies of life. The connection to the Lot of Fortune is vital: it situates "Necessity" in the realm of the body and circumstances. Necessity is not a choice; it is the physical or circumstantial framework within which the intellect (Mercury) must struggle. It signifies "constraints, submissions, struggles, and wars... and all other restrictive things befalling men".1

Historical Divergence: It is critical to note that Valens calculates Necessity as the arc from Spirit to Fortune (Day) or Fortune to Spirit (Night), without using Mercury. The Paulus formula is distinct because it incorporates the actual coordinate of Mercury.1 This makes the Paulus Lot of Necessity a "Planetary Lot" proper, whereas the Valens version is a structural point of the luminary relationship.
4.2 The Lot of Eros (Eros) — Venus
Greek Name: Eros (Love, Desire, Appetite).
Associated Planet: Venus (Aphrodite).
Anchor: Lot of Spirit.
Concept and Rationale: Venus represents union, harmony, and desire. The Panaretos anchors the Lot of Eros to the Lot of Spirit (Daimon). This is a profound philosophical statement: it asserts that true desire is an act of the will and the soul, distinct from the bodily necessities of Fortune. Eros signifies "appetites and desires occurring by choice," friendship, and mutual favor.7 By anchoring it to Spirit, the system classifies love as a voluntary movement of the soul, rather than a mere physical compulsion.

4.3 The Lot of Courage (Tolma) — Mars
Greek Name: Tolma (Daring, Boldness, Rashness, Audacity).
Associated Planet: Mars (Ares).
Anchor: Lot of Fortune.
Concept and Rationale: Mars is the lesser malefic, associated with heat, severance, action, and violence. The lot is named Tolma, implying a daring that can be either courageous or treacherous. It is anchored to the Lot of Fortune, suggesting that courage manifests in the physical world of circumstances. It is the capacity to act against fate or within the constraints of danger. Paulus describes it as a "contributing cause of boldness, treachery, might, and every villainy".7

6. Implementation Specifications and Worked Examples
For the technical writer and software engineer, abstract formulas must be translated into rigorous algorithms.
6.1 Computational Logic (Pseudocode Specification)
The following logic handles the circular normalization and the sect conditionals.


Python




# Constants for readability
SUN = 0
MOON = 1
MERCURY = 2
VENUS = 3
MARS = 4
JUPITER = 5
SATURN = 6
ASCENDANT = 7

def normalize(degree):
   """Normalize degrees to 0-360 range."""
   result = degree % 360
   if result < 0:
       result += 360
   return result

def get_sect(sun_long, asc_long):
   """
   Determine Sect.
   Simple geometric approximation:
   Day if Sun is between Asc and Dsc (0 to 180 degrees from Asc).
   """
   diff = normalize(sun_long - asc_long)
   if diff < 180:
       return "DAY"
   else:
       return "NIGHT"

def calculate_hermetic_lots(positions):
   """
   Calculate all 7 Hermetic Lots based on Paulus Alexandrinus.
   positions: Dictionary of planetary longitudes (0-360).
   """
   asc = positions
   sun = positions
   moon = positions['MOON']
   
   sect = get_sect(sun, asc)
   
   # 1. Primary Anchors (Fortune & Spirit)
   if sect == "DAY":
       fortune = normalize(asc + moon - sun)
       spirit = normalize(asc + sun - moon)
   else: # NIGHT
       fortune = normalize(asc + sun - moon)
       spirit = normalize(asc + moon - sun)
       
   lots = {
       'Fortune': fortune,
       'Spirit': spirit
   }
   
   # 2. Planetary Lots
   # Benefics (Venus/Jupiter) -> Anchor: Spirit
   # Malefics+Merc (Mars/Saturn/Mercury) -> Anchor: Fortune
   
   # Eros (Venus)
   # Day: Spirit -> Venus (Venus - Spirit)
   # Night: Venus -> Spirit (Spirit - Venus)
   if sect == "DAY":
       lots['Eros'] = normalize(asc + positions - spirit)
   else:
       lots['Eros'] = normalize(asc + spirit - positions)

   # Victory (Jupiter)
   # Day: Spirit -> Jupiter (Jupiter - Spirit)
   # Night: Jupiter -> Spirit (Spirit - Jupiter)
   if sect == "DAY":
       lots['Victory'] = normalize(asc + positions - spirit)
   else:
       lots['Victory'] = normalize(asc + spirit - positions)

   # Necessity (Mercury)
   # Day: Mercury -> Fortune (Fortune - Mercury)
   # Night: Fortune -> Mercury (Mercury - Fortune)
   if sect == "DAY":
       lots['Necessity'] = normalize(asc + fortune - positions)
   else:
       lots['Necessity'] = normalize(asc + positions - fortune)

   # Courage (Mars)
   # Day: Mars -> Fortune (Fortune - Mars)
   # Night: Fortune -> Mars (Mars - Fortune)
   if sect == "DAY":
       lots['Courage'] = normalize(asc + fortune - positions)
   else:
       lots['Courage'] = normalize(asc + positions - fortune)

   # Nemesis (Saturn)
   # Day: Saturn -> Fortune (Fortune - Saturn)
   # Night: Fortune -> Saturn (Saturn - Fortune)
   if sect == "DAY":
       lots['Nemesis'] = normalize(asc + fortune - positions)
   else:
       lots['Nemesis'] = normalize(asc + positions - fortune)
       
   return lots

7. Interpretive Implications and Modern Reconstruction
The meticulous restoration of these formulas allows for a deeper engagement with the "Web of Fate" as envisioned by the Alexandrian astrologers. When a modern chart calculator uses these correct vectors, distinct patterns emerge that are lost in the simplified Valens methods.
                                                                                                                  1. The Interdependence of Fate: In the Paulus system, one cannot know the position of Nemesis (Retribution) without first knowing the position of Fortune (Chance). This mathematically binds the concept of retribution to the physical circumstances of the native. One does not suffer Nemesis in a vacuum; one suffers it through the body and material assets.
                                                                                                                  2. The Volition of Love: By anchoring Eros to Spirit, the system argues that love is a function of the Daimon—the guiding spirit or higher intellect. This aligns with the Platonic conception of Eros as a divine madness that leads the soul upward, rather than a mere biological impulse (which would belong to Fortune).
                                                                                                                  3. Sect as Determinant: The reversal of formulas emphasizes the binary nature of time in Hellenistic thought. The "Night" is not just the absence of Sun; it is a distinct metaphysical state where the flow of influence reverses. In the Day, the Spirit projects outward to Victory; in the Night, the expansive principle of Jupiter flows back toward the Spirit.
By adhering to the Panaretos formulas—specifically the use of the planets themselves and the strict observation of sect and anchor points—the historian and practitioner align their work with the most rigorous technical tradition of late antiquity. This reconstruction clears away the confusion of medieval transmission and restores the Seven Hermetic Lots to their original geometric precision.

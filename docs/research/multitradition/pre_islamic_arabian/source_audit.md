# Pre-Islamic Arabian Anwāʾ and Seasonal Star-Lore Source Audit

Status: historical reconstruction is feasible; no birth-reading procedure is established  
Audit date: 2026-07-31  
Product status: `source_limited`; no natal customer reading is authorized

## Decision

The surviving evidence supports a historically valuable seasonal star,
weather, pastoral and agricultural reconstruction. It does not support a
generic pre-Islamic Arabian natal horoscope.

`Anwāʾ` must be separated into evidence layers:

1. pre-Islamic North Arabian inscriptions and material evidence;
2. pre-Islamic poetry and rhymed sayings preserved through later transmission;
3. early Islamic lexicographical and philological compilations;
4. the later systematic twenty-eight lunar-station framework;
5. region-specific Arabian and Yemeni seasonal calendars; and
6. Islamicate lunar-mansion astrology developed in other genres.

Later writers preserve older material but also reorganize, theologize and
systematize it. A medieval Arabic statement that “the Arabs said” is a source
claim requiring its own chain and comparison, not transparent access to a
single pre-Islamic system.

## Central primary-source compilation

The Max Planck Institute's ISMI catalog record for Ibn Qutayba's ninth-century
*Kitāb al-Anwāʾ* identifies the Arabic work, four manuscript witnesses and the
1956 Hyderabad edition:
<https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/text/116262>. The listed witnesses are in
Baghdad, two Cairo collections and the Bodleian Library.

Open Library independently catalogs the 1956 Hyderabad printing as *Kitāb
al-Anwāʾ fī mawāsim al-ʿArab*:
<https://openlibrary.org/books/OL3152735M/Kita%CC%84b_al-Anwa%CC%84%CA%BC_fi%CC%84_mawa%CC%84sim_al-%CA%BBArab>.

This is the first acquisition target because the complete text survives and
collects star names, risings/settings, weather, seasons and related lore. It is
not pre-Islamic primary evidence in date: Ibn Qutayba died in 889 CE. Each
embedded poem, saying, informant attribution and author explanation must be
tagged separately.

## The twenty-eight-station problem

Daniel Martin Varisco's 1991 “The Origin of the Anwāʾ in Arab Tradition,”
*Studia Islamica* 74, pp. 5-28, DOI 10.2307/1595894, is a central source-
critical study:
<https://relbib.de/Record/1688663657>. His work and subsequent scholarship
challenge a simple claim that a uniform twenty-eight-station lunar zodiac was
already the indigenous pre-Islamic system.

Danielle K. Adams's 2018 University of Arizona dissertation, *Rain Stars Set,
Lunar Stations Rise*, supplies a more recent full reconstruction framework:
<https://hdl.handle.net/10150/630173>. Its abstract distinguishes irregular,
observed and overlapping Arabian rain-star/seasonal complexes from a later
uniform lunar-station grid. Her 2021 chapter further traces how early Islamic
authors reframed rain-star agency and organized material as lunar stations:
<https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.4324/9780367407995-7/early-islamic-encounters-rain-stars-pre-islamic-arabian-astronomy-danielle-adams>.

This disagreement is architecturally decisive. The engine may offer:

- a source-specific Ibn Qutayba station/saying concordance; and
- a source-critical reconstruction of earlier rain-star complexes.

It may not label the later 28 equal sectors “the pre-Islamic Arabian zodiac” or
assign a person a station from their birthday without procedural evidence.

## Direct pre-Islamic evidence

The Online Corpus of the Inscriptions of Ancient North Arabia (OCIANA) is the
primary discovery corpus:
<https://ociana.osu.edu/>. It supplies photographs or facsimiles,
transliterations, translations, provenance, commentary and bibliography for
Ancient North Arabian inscriptions. The Oxford project description confirms
that its scope includes pre-Islamic north and central Arabia and Old Arabic:
<https://krc.web.ox.ac.uk/article/ociana>.

OCIANA enables direct testing of seasonal vocabulary, rain prayers, star names
and observational claims. It does not by itself prove that a medieval anwāʾ
list existed unchanged centuries earlier. Every inscription used must retain
corpus ID, script/language, object, provenance, photograph, reading history and
dating range.

Ahmad Al-Jallad's open-access *The Religion and Rituals of the Nomads of
Pre-Islamic Arabia* reconstructs Safaitic practice from the inscriptions:
<https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1163/j.ctv2kqwzwp>. It is essential cultural
context and a guide to primary records, not a natal manual.

North Arabian, South Arabian, Safaitic, Dadanitic, Nabataean and later Arabic
materials must not be collapsed into one ethnic or calendar unit. Evidence
from Yemen needs its own regional audit.

## What anwāʾ actually requires

The historical system centers on observable stellar phenomena and seasonal
conditions. A reproducible record may require:

- named star or asterism with source-specific identification;
- observed phenomenon: heliacal/acronychal rising, dawn setting or another
  explicitly defined appearance;
- geographic horizon and observing location;
- epoch, precession model and visibility assumptions;
- season and local ecological context;
- attributed rain, wind, heat, cold, pasture, animal or agricultural saying;
- source date and transmission layer; and
- whether a statement treats the star as sign, cause, agent or mnemonic.

Modern equatorial coordinates can help test an identification, but they must
not replace the source's horizon event or force an asterism into a zodiacal
longitude box.

## Valid-input map

| Technique family | Required input | Birth-only capability |
|---|---|---|
| Historical star/saying concordance | Source, star phenomenon, season and region | No |
| Seasonal calendar reconstruction | Region, epoch and observational horizon | No |
| Weather/pasture sayings | Current or historical season/location plus source | No; historical information only |
| Twenty-eight-station later concordance | Named medieval text and its station method | Not a natal reading unless source says so |
| Pre-Islamic inscription exploration | Corpus object, reading and archaeological context | No |
| Natal personality or fate reading | No verified procedure | Not supported |

The birth-intake product must therefore show this tradition as
`not_applicable_to_birth_data`, explain why, and offer a historical exploration
only if the user deliberately selects it. “Every possible reading” includes
honestly reporting that a tradition did not use this input for such a reading.

## First valid pilots

### Pilot A: Ibn Qutayba concordance

1. Acquire and hash the 1956 Arabic edition and lawful images of all four
   cataloged manuscripts.
2. Transcribe the complete star, phenomenon, season and weather sections.
3. Tag quotations, poetry, informants, Ibn Qutayba's prose and later editorial
   reconstruction separately.
4. Collate star order, variant names and rain-period claims across witnesses.
5. Have an Arabic philologist review all normalized terms and translations.

### Pilot B: observational reconstruction

1. Select five star events securely identified in both the philological corpus
   and direct or early comparative evidence.
2. Recompute their visible rising/setting at several plausible Arabian
   locations and historical epochs with explicit extinction and horizon
   assumptions.
3. Compare event dates with transmitted seasonal descriptions without forcing
   thirteen-day bins.
4. Preserve negative results where the sky event does not match a modern star
   identification or proposed regular grid.

### Pilot C: inscription/source concordance

1. Query OCIANA for seasonal, rain and star terminology.
2. Inspect every underlying image and reading history.
3. Compare only securely dated, geographically relevant inscriptions with
   later reported sayings.
4. Mark direct continuity, plausible analogy and unsupported retrojection as
   different evidence states.

## Safety and cultural limits

Weather lore is historical and cannot be presented as a reliable modern
forecast, agricultural instruction or emergency guidance. Rain-star agency and
religious language must be described respectfully within its source context,
not asserted as fact or caricatured. Ritual requests, divination or astral
agency are not customer instructions.

No person should receive deterministic personality, religion, fate, health,
marriage or wealth claims from a birthday-derived “Arabian lunar mansion.” The
evidence inspected here does not authorize that product.

## Failure conditions

The track remains `source_limited` if:

- a ninth-century compilation is called unmediated pre-Islamic evidence;
- the later 28-station grid is projected backward without qualification;
- equal zodiac sectors replace observed horizon phenomena;
- medieval Islamicate mansion astrology is relabeled pre-Islamic Arabian;
- North Arabian, South Arabian and Yemeni systems are merged;
- poetry, lexicography, inscription and modern reconstruction lose their
  evidence-layer identity;
- a birth date is converted into a natal reading without a source procedure;
- weather sayings become present-day forecasts or advice;
- no Arabic and Ancient North Arabian specialists review the corpus; or
- the interface implies a live Arabian engine while only Western is live.

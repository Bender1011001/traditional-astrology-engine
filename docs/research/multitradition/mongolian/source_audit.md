# Mongolian Zurkhai and Astrological Manuscript Source Audit

Status: source-limited for natal readings; bounded manuscript and electional research is feasible  
Audit date: 2026-07-31  
Product status: `source_limited`; no customer reading is authorized

## Decision

Mongolian `zurkhai` is a real, technically rich family of calendrical,
astronomical, astrological and divinatory practices. The inspected evidence
does not yet justify a generic Mongolian birth chart. It does justify a
carefully separated research program for:

1. Mongolian-language manuals;
2. Tibetan-language Buddhist astral works copied and used in Mongolia;
3. Mongolian calendar schools and regional adaptations;
4. electional and activity-specific day judgment;
5. natal or birth-derived calculations, if verified in a procedural source;
6. Kalmyk, Buryat and Inner Mongolian descendants as separate living lineages.

The existing Tibetan audit can supply comparative mathematics and source
terminology. It cannot be relabeled Mongolian. A shared Kalachakra ancestor is
evidence for a relationship, not identity of constants, epochs, calendar
schools or interpretations.

## Strongest Mongolian-language technical leads

### Mostaert and Cleaves, 1969

The major published lead is Antoine Mostaert's *Manual of Mongolian Astrology
and Divination*, edited by Francis Woodman Cleaves, Harvard University Press,
1969. The inspected bibliographic pages identify 127 pages in the *Scripta
Mongolica* series:
<https://books.google.com/books?hl=en&id=L2FkAAAAMAAJ> and
<https://search.worldcat.org/title/manual-of-mongolian-astrology-and-divination/oclc/116383>.

The visible index vocabulary points to lunar mansions, activities, favorable
and unfavorable days, planets and calendrical categories, but the complete
book was not inspected. It is the first acquisition priority because it may
provide an edited Mongolian text, translation and source description suitable
for passage-level work. No rule may be inferred from snippets.

### Ernst Collection item 139

Karénina Kollmar-Paulenz's 2025 open catalogue supplies an unusually precise
manuscript record:
<https://books.unibe.ch/index.php/BB/catalog/download/44/1017/2216?inline=1>.
Item 139, `GDC Mong M2 : 3 / ET_875`, is titled *Shira jiruqai-yin yosun bui:
chagh ularil-un toghan-u ujelge*. The catalogue records fifteen folios plus
three additional folios, Mongolian pagination, diagrams, incipit and explicit,
and classifies it as a manual of favorable and unfavorable days for activities
through the four seasons.

This is an excellent bounded electional witness. It does not yet establish a
birth-reading method. Complete images, a diplomatic transcription and a
parallel witness are required before encoding its day conditions.

## Mongolian provenance versus textual language

The U.S. National Library of Medicine describes a nineteenth-century
illustrated *Manual of Astrology and Divination* from Mongolia:
<https://circulatingnow.nlm.nih.gov/2014/11/13/mongolian-manuscript-comes-to-turning-the-pages/>.
Indexed curatorial material says that its text is Tibetan and that its diagrams
support planetary, eclipse and auspicious-activity calculations in a Mongolian
Buddhist environment. Direct access returned HTTP 429 during this audit, so the
record remains `search_metadata_only`.

The distinction is essential:

- provenance: copied, owned or used in Mongolia;
- language: Tibetan;
- intellectual lineages: described as Indian, Tibetan and Chinese influenced;
- local practice: potentially Mongolian, but not yet isolated passage by
  passage.

The manuscript may become an important Mongolian-use recension after Tibetan
and Mongolian specialists compare it with its textual ancestors. It is not
proof that every diagram is uniquely Mongolian.

## Calendar-school problem

The inspected abstract of "Types and Characteristics of the 18th Century
Mongolian Astronomy" describes `zurkhai` as a combined astronomical,
astrological and calendrical field and emphasizes regional Mongolian
adaptations after Chinese and Tibetan/Indian transmission:
<https://scholar.kyobobook.co.kr/article/detail/4010028402324>. It identifies
the eighteenth century as a period of multiple publications and systematized
theories and points to *Merged garakhin oron*.

This supports a multiple-school architecture. It does not yet provide the
arithmetic for the modern Tögs Buyant calendar or prove which historical
calendar a manuscript used. The Mongolian engine must record:

- named school and source work;
- epoch and geographical reference;
- intercalary month and repeated/omitted lunar-day rules;
- weekday, lunar mansion and planetary procedures;
- conversion between recorded Mongolian, Tibetan, Chinese and Gregorian dates;
- whether the output follows traditional tables or modern astronomy; and
- regional almanac and New Year decisions.

No result should be cached merely under `mongolian`. The school/version is part
of calculation identity.

## Corpus discovery

Inner Mongolia University Library's Mongolian Digital Library reports 13,115
ancient works and supports Mongolian, Chinese and Latin title search:
<https://nom.imu.edu.cn/en/ancientworks/>. This is a high-value discovery
repository, but its landing page is not astrology evidence. Searches must use
Mongolian script, multiple transliterations and named authors, then verify each
item's language, edition and images.

The open Ernst Collection catalogue is immediately actionable because its
entries include manuscript structure and transcribed boundaries. Broader
catalogs at national, Russian, Danish and U.S. institutions remain acquisition
routes, not rule authorities until an item is opened.

## Living-lineage evidence

The University of Cambridge Kalmyk Cultural Heritage Documentation Project
record for Yuriy Nurdaev documents uses of Mongolian astrology for choosing
days for rituals, holidays, construction, weddings, funerals and hunting, and
for weather forecasting:
<https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/bd766c8c-0b0e-46dd-98ea-dfa37ca78444>.
The record notes Chinese and Tibetan influence. The 8.68 GB interview was not
viewed, and its CC BY-NC-ND license forbids derivative and commercial reuse.

Kalmyk evidence must remain Kalmyk unless reviewers demonstrate a shared rule.
The same separation applies to Buryat, Mongolian state, Inner Mongolian and
other regional practitioners. Living-practice interviews are validation and
cultural context, not public-domain rulebooks.

## Birth-input capability map

| Technique family | Can one birth record drive it? | Current disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Calendar identity and animal/element year | Probably, after a school-specific date conversion | Calendar school and boundary rules unverified |
| Birth-derived numerology/directional systems | Reported in modern practice, but no controlling source inspected | `source_limited` |
| Full natal judgment | Not established by inspected evidence | No output |
| Favorable/unfavorable activity days | Requires activity and candidate date; may also use a person's birth category | Separate electional workflow |
| Weather, funeral and ritual divination | Not a natal reading | Separate culturally reviewed historical modules |
| Planet/eclipses diagrams | Astronomical or calendrical inputs, not necessarily birth data | Source-specific research only |

## First valid pilots

### Pilot A: Mongolian-language seasonal electional manual

1. Acquire complete images of Ernst Collection item 139.
2. Transcribe all folios in Mongolian script and normalized transliteration.
3. Translate every activity, calendrical condition, exception and judgment.
4. Identify the required calendar school and reconstruct its day state.
5. Compare at least one parallel manuscript or almanac.
6. Produce a deterministic trace for one activity/date question, not a natal
   personality reading.

### Pilot B: Mostaert source concordance

1. Lawfully acquire the 1969 volume.
2. Identify and locate its source witness.
3. Separate edition text, French/English explanation, editor reconstruction and
   manuscript content.
4. Inventory which rules use birth information and which are event/electional.
5. Promote only precisely cited, independently reviewed procedures.

A calendar pilot follows only after the named Mongolian school, source tables
and boundary examples are acquired.

## Safety and cultural limits

The NLM corpus prominently intersects health, death handling and ritual. These
materials may be historically documented but cannot become medical advice,
instructions for treating illness, claims of literal supernatural causation or
coercive ritual prescriptions. Funeral and post-mortem material requires
special cultural review. Living interview material must remain within its
license and community context.

## Failure conditions

The track remains `source_limited` if:

- a Tibetan-language Mongolian manuscript is called Mongolian-language;
- Tibetan calendar constants are reused without a Mongolian school concordance;
- Mongolian, Kalmyk, Buryat and Inner Mongolian practices are merged;
- electional day rules are converted into natal personality meanings;
- animal-year or popular web meanings substitute for a primary procedure;
- the calendar epoch, intercalation, omitted/repeated day and location are
  hidden;
- no Mongolian-language and lineage reviewer approves the trace;
- health, death, weather or ritual claims are presented as factual advice; or
- the interface implies a live Mongolian reading while only the Western engine
  is currently implemented.

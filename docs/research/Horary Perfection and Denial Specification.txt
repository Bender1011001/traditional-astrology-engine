The Algorithms of Fate: A Formal Specification of Perfection and Denial in Horary Astrology
1. Introduction: The Horary Chart as a Deterministic State Machine
In the specialized domain of horary astrology, the celestial sphere is not treated as a chaotic or purely poetic expanse, but rather as a rigorous, logical mechanism—a finite state machine governed by deterministic rules of motion and interaction. When a practitioner casts a chart for a specific query, they are effectively initializing a simulation environment. The planets act as variable agents, the zodiacal signs function as state containers modifying those agents, and the houses serve as the topological domain mapping celestial coordinates to terrestrial contexts. The objective of the horary specialist, functioning much like a formal methods engineer, is to traverse the graph of planetary states to determine if a specific connection condition—termed "Perfection"—can be satisfied, or if the system logic dictates an exception or failure state, known as "Denial."
This research report provides a comprehensive, programmatic specification of these mechanics. It eschews the vague mysticism often associated with popular astrology in favor of the precise, algorithmic definitions found in the primary source texts of the tradition, principally William Lilly’s Christian Astrology (1647), and his medieval antecedents such as Masha’allah and Guido Bonatti. We analyze the "Motion of Light" doctrines—Translation, Collection, Prohibition, Refranation, and Frustration—not merely as aphorisms, but as distinct computational subroutines that govern the flow of causality within the chart.
The central premise of this specification is that the horary chart operates on a "Physics of Light." Light, in this context, is the information carrier—the signal transmitted by a Significator (a planet representing a person or event). The successful delivery of this signal from the Querent’s Significator to the Quesited’s Significator constitutes the "Perfection" of the matter. However, this signal is subject to interference, interception, diversion, and signal decay. The Motion of Light doctrines are the protocols that describe these signal behaviors. By rigorous application of these protocols, the astrologer derives a boolean output: True (the event happens) or False (the event is denied), often accompanied by a complex return value describing the manner of the outcome (e.g., "Yes, but with delay," or "No, because of a third party").
1.1 The Ontological Primitives: Planets as Agents
To understand the mechanics of perfection, one must first define the agents operating within the system. In the horary model, the seven visible planets (Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon) are the active variables. Each planet possesses a set of dynamic properties that determine its "privilege" or capacity to act within the algorithm.
1. Longitude (  ): The precise position of the planet on the ecliptic (0° to 360°).
2. Velocity (  ): The rate of change of longitude. This determines which planet is the "Applicant" (active) and which is the "Recipient" (passive).
3. Moiety (  ): The radius of the planet's "Orb of Influence," representing the spatial tolerance for interactions.
4. Dignity (  ): A composite score derived from the planet's sign position (Domicile, Exaltation, Triplicity, Term, Face), determining its "power" or "virtue."
5. Motion State: Whether the planet is Direct, Retrograde, or Stationary.
The interaction of these primitives creates the complex behaviors of Translation and Prohibition. For instance, a planet’s velocity is not constant; it accelerates and decelerates. A "Prohibition" logic gate may evaluate to True only because the interfering planet’s velocity allows it to intercept the target before the primary significator arrives. Thus, the system is dynamic, requiring calculating temporal trajectories rather than just static geometric relationships.

2. The Geometry of Interaction: Orbs, Moiety, and the Aspectual Field
Before any "Motion of Light" routine can be executed, the system must validate the existence of a connection path. In modern astrology, aspects are often treated as dimensionless vectors (lines) with an arbitrary tolerance (orb). In traditional horary mechanics, as formalized by Lilly and Bonatti, planets are treated as bodies with volumetric fields of influence. The logic of contact is governed by the theory of Moiety.
2.1 The Theory of Moiety and Planetary Radii
The term "Moiety" is derived from the Old French moitié, meaning half. It refers to the radius of the planet's orb—the distance from the planet's center to the edge of its effective field. An aspect is not a connection between two centers, but an overlapping of two fields.
The condition for a "Platic" (wide) aspect—the initial state of connection—is met when the distance between the aspectual points of two planets is less than or equal to the sum of their moieties.

2.2 The Handshake Protocol: Application vs. Separation
The binary state of Application (Applicatio) versus Separation (Separatio) is the most critical logic gate in the system.
   * Separation (  ): Occurs when the faster planet is moving away from the exact aspect of the slower planet.
      * Logical Implication: The event has passed. The opportunity is closed. In a query about a future event, a separating aspect returns a value of False for perfection (unless a "Return of Light" occurs).
      * Context: Separation contains the "memory" of the chart. It tells the analyst what just happened—the context of the question.
      * Application (  ): Occurs when the faster planet is moving toward the exact aspect of the slower planet.
         * Logical Implication: The event is pending. The energy is building. This is the domain of potential Perfection.
         * Requirement: The application must be continuous. The state of application persists from the moment the moieties touch until the moment of exact partile perfection.
Directional Vector:
The flow of influence is strictly unidirectional in terms of initiation. The lighter (faster) planet applies to the heavier (slower) planet.
         * Query: "Will I (Mercury) get the job (Saturn)?"
         * Valid State: Mercury applies to Saturn. (The applicant petitions the boss).
         * Invalid State: Saturn applies to Mercury. (This is physically impossible unless Saturn is Retrograde or Mercury is incredibly slow/stationary).
This vector establishes the "Agency" of the chart. The faster planet represents the active party seeking the outcome. If the Querent is the faster planet, they must act. If the Quesited is the faster planet, the "thing" is coming to the Querent.2

10. Conclusion
The "Perfection and Denial" mechanics of horary astrology constitute a sophisticated system of causal logic. Far from being a random collection of omens, the chart functions as a deterministic simulation of interacting agents. The "Motion of Light" doctrines—Translation, Collection, Prohibition, Frustration, and Refranation—act as the control structures (loops, interrupts, and conditionals) of this simulation.
For the formal methods engineer, the horary chart is a solvable equation. By rigorously defining the Moieties (fields of influence), tracking the vectors of Application, and executing the subroutines of interference and mediation, one can derive a consistent, reproducible judgment. The system honors the complexity of human fate by acknowledging that while the shortest path (Direct Perfection) is desired, the universe often requires the circuitous routes of Translation and Collection, or imposes the hard stops of Prohibition and Refranation. The mastery of horary lies in the precise calculation of these celestial algorithms.

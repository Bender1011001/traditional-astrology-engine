The Calculus of Fate: A Comprehensive Catalog and Analysis of Hellenistic and Persian Astrological Lots
1. Introduction
The history of horoscopic astrology is underpinned by a tension between the observation of physical celestial bodies and the calculation of immaterial, geometric points. These points, known in the Greek tradition as kleroi (Lots) and in the Arabic tradition as siham (Shares or Parts), represent a distinct metaphysical technology. Unlike planets, which possess mass and visible light, Lots are purely mathematical constructs—syntheses of planetary arcs projected onto the local horizon. They serve as phantom coordinates where specific topics (wealth, marriage, war, barley, wheat) are said to manifest in the life of the native.
This report provides an exhaustive, source-faithful catalog and analysis of these Lots, restricting its scope to two foundational texts that bookend the transmission of this technique from Late Antiquity to the Islamic Golden Age: the Introductory Matters (Eisagogika) of the 4th-century Hellenistic astrologer Paulus Alexandrinus, and the Book of Instruction in the Elements of the Art of Astrology (Kitāb al-Tafhīm) by the 11th-century Persian polymath Abū al-Rayḥān al-Bīrūnī.
While modern astrological practice has largely reduced this system to a single point—the Lot of Fortune—the historical reality is a vast, intricate algorithm of destiny. Paulus Alexandrinus presents the Lots as a "Hermetic" heptad, a closed system of seven planetary lots designed to map the soul's descent into matter and its volitional struggles.1 Seven centuries later, al-Bīrūnī documents an explosion of this technique, cataloging nearly a hundred lots that extend the mathematical logic to the minutiae of agrarian and social life, from the price of cotton to the fidelity of friends.2
The following analysis treats these Lots primarily as mathematical constructions. By isolating the variables (Significator, Trigger, and Anchor) and analyzing the sect-based logic of their formulas, we reveal a sophisticated system of "celestial algebra" suitable for rigorous scholarly reference and computational modeling.

2. Theoretical Framework and Computational Logic
Before cataloging individual Lots, it is necessary to establish the computational and doctrinal framework that governs their construction in both the Hellenistic and Persian traditions.
2.1 The Geometry of Projection
Every Lot is constructed via a tripartite formula involving three points:
1. Point A (Start): The trigger or cause.
2. Point B (End): The significator or effect.
3. Point C (Anchor): The point of projection (almost invariably the Ascendant).
The formula calculates the arc distance from A to B (measured in the order of signs) and adds this arc to the longitude of C.
  

Note: If the result is negative, 360° is added. If it exceeds 360°, 360° is subtracted.
This geometry is not arbitrary. It takes a "celestial phase" (the relationship between two planets) and grounds it in the "terrestrial moment" (the Ascendant). If the Sun and Moon are 90° apart in the sky, the Lot of Fortune will always be 90° from the Ascendant. The Lot thereby replicates the planetary aspect on the framework of the local horizon.
2.2 The Doctrine of Sect (Hairesis)
A critical distinction between modern simplifications and the source texts is the treatment of Sect—the distinction between Day charts (Sun above the horizon) and Night charts (Sun below the horizon).
* Paulus Alexandrinus: Rigorously applies sect reversal. For Paulus, the "Diurnal" formula is valid only when the Sun is above the earth. At night, the formula reverses (   becomes   ) to reflect the change in the "sect light" (Luminary of the time).4
* Al-Bīrūnī: Al-Bīrūnī affirms this necessity, criticizing those who neglect it. His catalog explicitly lists separate instructions for "Diurnal" and "Nocturnal" calculations, noting that for night births, "points 1 and 2 are interchanged".6
2.3 Primary vs. Derived Variables
The system is hierarchical.
   * Primary Variables: The Ascendant, Sun, and Moon. These generate the two "Master Lots" (Fortune and Spirit).
   * Derived Variables: The Lots of Fortune and Spirit themselves become variables in subsequent formulas. For example, the Lot of Eros in the Hermetic system is not derived from the Ascendant directly, but from the relationship between Spirit and Venus. This creates a dependency chain in computational modeling: Fortune and Spirit must be calculated before the planetary lots.7

4. The Hermetic Heptad: The Planetary Lots of Paulus
Paulus Alexandrinus transmits a specific, closed system of seven Lots attributed to Hermes Trismegistus, known as the Panaretus. These are strictly "Planetary Lots," assigning one lot to each of the five non-luminary planets.
Crucial Distinction: Unlike later Arabic simplifications (which often used simple    formulas), the Hermetic Lots of Paulus integrate the metaphysical variables of Fortune and Spirit into the planetary calculations. This binds the planets to the fundamental Soul/Body duality.

6. Interpretive Protocol: The Lord of the Lot
A Lot is merely a point in the zodiac. Its power to act is entirely dependent on its Lord (Oikodespotes). Both Paulus and al-Bīrūnī adhere to a strict protocol for analyzing the Lord, which acts as the "Steward" of the Lot's topics.
6.1 Identifying the Lord
                                                               1. Calculate the longitude of the Lot.
                                                               2. Identify the Domicile Ruler of that sign (e.g., Lot in Gemini    Mercury).
                                                               3. Secondary: Al-Bīrūnī may also consider the Exaltation ruler or Term ruler, but the Domicile is the primary "Lord."
6.2 The Condition of the Lord (Paulus Ch. 24)
The standard of the Lord determines the quality of the Lot's manifestation.
                                                                  1. Angularity (The Stakes):
                                                                  * If the Lord is in an Angle (1st, 4th, 7th, 10th) or Succedent (5th, 11th) house, the lot is active, effective, and "loud."
                                                                  * If the Lord is Cadent (3rd, 6th, 9th, 12th) or in "Declining" places, the lot is weak, obscure, or brings its topics through difficulty.11
                                                                  2. The "10th Place" Rule (Eminence):
                                                                  * This is the "Gold Standard" for the Lot of Fortune.
                                                                  * Rule: If the Lord of Fortune is located in the 10th Sign counted from Fortune itself, the native attains exceptional eminence, power, and high office (praxis).
                                                                  * Example: Fortune is in Aries. The 10th sign from Aries is Capricorn. If Mars (Lord of Aries) is in Capricorn, the condition is met. This is considered more powerful than the Lord being in the 10th house from the Ascendant.5
                                                                  3. Witnessing and Maltreatment:
                                                                  * Witnessing: The Lord must "see" (aspect) its own Lot. A Lord in aversion (unconjunct) to its Lot indicates that the topics of the Lot (e.g., wealth) slide away or cannot be managed by the native.
                                                                  * Maltreatment: If the Lot or its Lord is squared/opposed by Malefics (Mars/Saturn) without the intervention of Benefics, the topic ends in ruin.
                                                                  * Enclosure: Enclosure of the Lord by Benefics (Venus/Jupiter) is the highest protection.24
6.3 Functional Roles
                                                                  * The Lot is the Trigger: It signifies the topic (e.g., "Wheat" or "Marriage").
                                                                  * The Lord is the Outcome: It signifies the result (e.g., "Abundant harvest" or "Difficult divorce").
                                                                  * Synthesis: A Lot of Wheat in Taurus (fertile) with its Lord Venus in Virgo (fall/cadent) suggests a good potential crop (Taurus) that is ruined by poor management or pests (debilitated Lord).

8. Conclusion and Synthesis
The system of Lots represents a "Shadow Zodiac"—a geometric overlay that personalizes the universal planetary positions.
For the Hellenistic tradition (Paulus), Lots are a mechanism of the Soul's incarnation. By triangulating the Ascendant (Existence), the Sun (Spirit), and the Moon (Body), Paulus creates a map of "Necessity" and "Volition." The strict adherence to sect reversal underscores a dualistic worldview where Day and Night operate as fundamental metaphysical states.
For the Persian tradition (al-Bīrūnī), the Lots become a mechanism of Universal Measurement. The expansion into commodities suggests a worldview where everything—from the soul's salvation to the price of lentils—is subject to the same celestial harmonic laws. The recurrence of formulas (e.g., Wheat = Male Children) indicates that al-Bīrūnī viewed the relationship between planets (e.g., Jupiter-Sun) as a fundamental archetype that manifests differently depending on the context (Agrarian vs. Familial).
Recommendation for Computational Implementation:
To model this system faithfully, software must:
                                                                  1. Implement Sect as a Boolean Operator: Every formula must check is_day_chart before calculation.
                                                                  2. Establish Dependency Trees: Calculate Fortune and Spirit first; pass them as variables to the Hermetic Lots.
                                                                  3. Differentiate Homonyms: Distinctly label "Lot of Eros (Paulus)" and "Lot of Love (Al-Bīrūnī)" to avoid confusion.
                                                                  4. Implement the 10th Place Logic: An automated analysis must check the condition of the Lord relative to the Lot's position, not just the Ascendant.
By respecting these distinctions, one preserves the integrity of a technique that served for over a millennium as the primary tool for calculating the specificities of human fate.
